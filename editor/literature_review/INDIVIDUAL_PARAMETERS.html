Individual sigmoid parameters and common RT plateaus

Evidence checked 16 October 2026. This is an exploratory extension requested after the original participant fit gate failed. Four Condition × Phase curves per person remain the primary specification. All numerical results and the complete method comparison are generated in the browser; the original gated hypotheses remain unestimated. The analysis contract records the protocol and subsequent amendments.

Scientific motivation and limits

The PPS evidence review provides both sigmoid precedents and contrary evidence about expectancy and response shape. Those findings motivate a candidate model, rather than a requirement that these data follow it. The earlier review also explains local regression, identifiability and nested prediction.

Ahn et al. (2011) report that hierarchical Bayesian estimation improved parameter recovery over individual- or group-level maximum likelihood in an Iowa Gambling Task simulation. This motivates examining partial pooling and recovery here. Our estimator is a penalized finite-grid point fit with bootstrap uncertainty, not that paper's Bayesian model. Its penalty, bandwidth and visible-shape constraints are explicitly exploratory choices.

Hedge, Powell and Sumner (2018) show that robust group effects in common cognitive tasks need not yield reliable individual differences for correlation studies. This supports assessing reliability directly before interpreting PPS–MAIA links. Their results do not determine the reliability of this experiment; our split-half and recovery analyses address it with their own limited scope. Random split halves assess within-session stability, not test–retest reliability, and simulation recovery depends on its assumed signal and empirical residual distribution.

What normalization does

For RT(d) = b + a expit((d − m)/s), define the lower plateau L = min(b, b+a) and separation A = |a|. Then normalized_RT = (RT − L)/A. The fitted lower and upper plateaus are exactly 0 and 1. This affine transformation preserves midpoint m and width s and retains decreasing curves. Normalized steepness magnitude is 1/(4s) per cm; the original signed RT derivative is a/(4s) ms/cm. These are different estimands. This algebra is our derivation, not a claim borrowed from the cited papers.

The plateaus are fitted asymptotes, not necessarily observed endpoints. Observed RTs can lie outside 0–1 and are never clipped. Flat curves cannot be normalized; an amplitude interval containing zero flags an unstable denominator. Scaling a weak response to unit amplitude can make it look strong, so every participant's atlas page places raw means and uncertainty above the normalized model-only geometry. Shared axes and identical panel sizes make the four shapes comparable.

Questionnaire links and recording integrity

MAIA-2 is a baseline trait; spatial ratings follow each intervention. The main links correlate each of eight subscales with the Viscereality−Control parameter difference (equal phase weights), and correlate that difference with the numerical spatial-rating difference. Four separate curves remain the fitted units; averaging their parameters to define a condition contrast does not replace those curves. There are 27 main comparisons per model, 96 supplementary cell-specific MAIA links, and three ordinal-direction sensitivities: 126 per model. Both plateau models and three recording-exclusion specifications give 1,008 reported unadjusted tests. Intervals are pointwise. No multiple-testing correction is applied, as requested. Point-estimate permutation p values condition on estimated proxies; hierarchical bootstrap intervals refit the parameters and therefore address additional uncertainty. They need not give matching significance decisions.

The audit found that P011 and P033 share all 408 behavioural records with intervention labels swapped, despite different questionnaire responses. Their original recording-to-person mapping must be resolved. Source CSVs are preserved; both curves are flagged. Linked copies are excluded together from each other's pool and kept together in outer folds. Excluding both, or each separately, repeats method selection and all associations. These sensitivities do not identify the correct mapping. Full-release behavioural inference and RT–questionnaire links are provisional; the questionnaire-only spatial/MAIA analysis does not use these RTs.

Completed remaining-analysis amendment

The browser now adds 500 refits for each recording-exclusion cohort, rather than leaving those associations at point estimates. It also reports all 72 cohort-mean Condition, Phase and interaction contrasts across two models and four linkage specifications. Every interval includes zero, as do all 216 main association intervals across those specifications. No multiplicity adjustment was introduced.

The changes used in correlations have weak split-half rank reliability. Median rho for midpoint change is −.074 with separate plateaus and .066 with shared plateaus; normalized-steepness change is .052 and −.009. Signed RT-slope change is .064 and .155. Known-signal contrast recovery is reported for both simulated plateau structures. A large model point difference can result from pooled shape centres without a well-identified physiological change; refitting uncertainty must accompany it. The protocol is a post-results extension, not a new confirmation.
