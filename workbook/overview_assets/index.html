Study 5: questions, analyses and evidence

Does breathing phase, the Viscereality intervention, or their interaction change peripersonal space (PPS)? We work through the measurement assumptions before interpreting experimental effects.

This living scientific overview follows a question-by-question sequence: define the data, examine the observed response, assess estimation and reliability, test effects, then explore associations. Each section separates the analysis from what the evidence allows us to conclude.

Working overview · 22 October 2026. The registration and later exploratory extensions remain distinct. This page summarizes existing evidence; it does not execute models. Browser analyses, native-only extensions and outstanding validation are identified below. Scientific review and publication readiness remain open.

1. Which data can answer our questions?

Analysis approach

Audit the released participant and trial tables, missingness, fixed cleaning and questionnaire scoring. Preserve the four within-person cells: Control/Viscereality × Exhale/Inhale.

Assumptions and checks

Participant records must represent independent recordings. P011 and P033 share the same 408 behavioural rows with condition labels swapped; the recording-to-person mapping is unresolved. Primary new behavioural inference excludes both, with full-release and single-exclusion sensitivities.

Current evidence

The audited release contains 42 participant records and 17,114 trial rows. Primary new behavioural inference uses 40 records. Exclusion protects the analysis from duplicated RT evidence but cannot resolve the missing source mapping. Belt compliance and waveform timing cannot be verified from these CSVs.

2. How do we isolate the Audio–Tactile response?

Analysis approach

Define corrected RT as Audio–Tactile RT − Baseline RT. The primary correction uses the baseline mean within participant × condition × phase. Keep raw RT as a reference and same-nominal-SOA subtraction as a timing-unverified sensitivity.

Assumptions and checks

Baseline is estimated with uncertainty. Reusing one mean across distances induces shared covariance; propagate it rather than treating corrected observations as independent. Check sparse baseline cells and translation invariance when a curve has a free intercept. Corrected RT can be negative.

Current evidence

The native corrected-model batch implements these checks. Same-nominal-SOA subtraction does not establish matched cue timing. Plateau normalization is a separate transformation and cannot substitute for baseline subtraction.

3. Do the observations support a sigmoid?

Analysis approach

Inspect trial-level responses and participant profiles. Compare constant, linear, log-distance and sigmoid predictions on held-out distances, keeping the validation targets unsmoothed. Separate observed curvature from a model's imposed S-shaped geometry.

Assumptions and checks

A sigmoid should earn its complexity through prediction and data information. Retain flat and reversed observations, examine prior-to-posterior updating, and check whether both bends are supported inside the measured 10–100 cm range.

Current evidence

The original trial-level R² ≥ .70 criterion passed in 0/168 cells. In the separate unpooled Bayesian reference, the sigmoid-minus-constant held-distance RMSE difference was −0.59 ms, with a paired-person 95% interval of [−2.45, 1.13] ms. That comparison does not establish a clear predictive advantage over a constant or identify a physiological boundary.

4. Can we estimate reliable individual boundaries?

Analysis approach

Estimate the midpoint m (cm) and normalized midpoint sharpness 1/(4w) (cm⁻¹) for each participant's four cells. Propagate parameter uncertainty, compare priors, examine split halves and test recovery with known signals.

Assumptions and checks

A finite posterior median is a model estimate, not proof of an identified physiological boundary. Distinguish normalized sharpness from RT amplitude and the signed RT derivative. Pooling can stabilize estimates while increasing dependence on shared assumptions.

Current evidence

Under the primary unpooled Bayesian reference, none of the 160 non-ambiguous cells had a midpoint 95% interval wholly inside 10–100 cm. The median midpoint interval spanned approximately 173 cm. Later joint, positive-direction and visible-S fits are separate native extensions; their constraints do not validate individual measurement.

5. Do condition and breathing phase change PPS?

Analysis approach

For both midpoint and normalized sharpness, report Phase (Inhale − Exhale), Condition (Viscereality − Control), and Condition × Phase. Preserve participant pairing and all six primary parameter contrasts.

Assumptions and checks

Propagate curve-estimation uncertainty and distinguish finite-cohort contrasts from population uncertainty. Retain prior and cohort sensitivities. Conventional ANOVAs on posterior medians are separately labelled because they omit within-fit uncertainty. Report the full comparison family; frequentist p values remain unadjusted.

Current evidence

The archived unpooled Bayesian browser reference did not establish a robust parameter difference; this is inconclusive evidence, not an equivalence result. Later native joint-model effects must be read with their own diagnostics and specifications. A general RT benefit or a simple phase effect does not itself establish a PPS boundary change or an interaction.

6. Do conclusions depend on analysis assumptions?

Analysis approach

Compare the declared baseline rules, model families, priors, linkage cohorts, trial filters, early-block selections, rolling means and continuous session-time adjustment. Preserve the original analysis alongside every sensitivity.

Assumptions and checks

Both interventions occur in one sitting, so global trial order must not restart by condition. Overlapping rolling means require their full covariance. Constraining all curves to one direction or forcing an in-range S is a theoretical assumption; neither is evidence discovered from the data.

Current evidence

These native sensitivity batches have separate specifications and results. Retain failed diagnostics, near-zero responses and the signed-model comparison. Early blocks are a fewer-trial sensitivity, not a verified fatigue exclusion. Do not choose cleaning, priors or cohorts by significance.

8. Are PPS estimates related to self-report?

Analysis approach

Explore all eight baseline MAIA-2 subscales and condition-matched post-intervention spatial ratings. Evaluate within-person spatial change (Viscereality − Control), ordinal sensitivities and exploratory moderation while propagating PPS parameter uncertainty.

Assumptions and checks

MAIA-2 was measured once before the experiment. Spatial ratings were measured once after each condition, not separately by breathing phase. Numerical category differences assume comparable spacing. The administered pictogram anchor key is missing, so higher codes cannot be labelled more allocentric or egocentric.

Current evidence

The browser-reference report did not establish a reliable PPS association after considering parameter and prior uncertainty. Retain all scales and findings, including separate spatial-rating results. Trait moderation and post-intervention associations do not demonstrate causal mediation.
