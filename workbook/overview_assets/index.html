2. Do baseline-corrected RTs differ between inhale and exhale?

Baseline-corrected RTs averaged −17.57 ms during exhale and −19.73 ms during inhale. Inhale − Exhale was −2.16 ms (95% CI −12.15 to 7.82 ms; p = .664): the corrected phase effect was inconclusive. Raw RT is an audit reference: exhale 662.19 ms, inhale 680.66 ms, or 18.47 ms slower during inhale (95% CI 6.96 to 29.98 ms; paired p = .0024).

Exploratory paired analysis of 40 participants, excluding P011/P033; equal weight across conditions and SOAs, then across participants. Two-sided p values are unadjusted. These are RT differences, not PPS boundary effects.

3. Did baseline-corrected RTs decline across blocks?

There was no clear systematic RT decline across the 12 chronological blocks spanning both consecutive conditions. For AT − matched Baseline RT, the average participant slope was −0.12 ms per block (95% CI −1.32 to 1.08; t(38) = −0.20, p = .840).
