Analysis rationale and claim boundaries

This document records the rationale for the existing exploratory analysis after the data and results were inspected. It does not change its registration status. Source identifiers refer to the evidence table.

Questions and estimands

The primary question remains the Condition × instructed Phase × SOA interaction in retained trial RTs. The exploratory question is different: how well can one common extraction/smoothing/model rule summarize four participant-weighted RT profiles, and how much do transition parameters depend on that rule?

For extractor T, the descriptive response at condition/phase g and position j is mean_i T(retained RTs in person i, g, j). Mean, median and winsorized versions are different population summaries. The midpoint of a curve through their group averages is generally not the average of individual midpoints. Inference across four plotted curves cannot replace inference across people.

All candidate scores use the same target: mean_RT(i,g,j) - mean_{k != j} mean_RT(i,g,k). Here mean_RT denotes an unsmoothed arithmetic mean of retained trials. Predictions are centered on the training group's four unsmoothed extracted cell summaries. This evaluates profile shape rather than absolute response speed, and the held-out person's other cells define the target rather than enter the fitted training curve. The constant comparator predicts zero contrast.

What the validation actually covers

Participant-wide mean ±2.5 sample-SD trimming is performed before these candidate comparisons. Its thresholds are calculated separately for each person from that person's eligible AT and Baseline hit RTs across all positions. No held-out person's RTs determine a training person's threshold. However, the omitted position can affect its own person's precomputed cleaning threshold. Therefore the current assessment is conditional on cleaned complete profiles, not prospective prediction with all preprocessing learned from the four available positions.

Inner selection is repeated within outer training participants; it still uses all five positions in those training people. The design coordinates used in shape constraints are known in advance. These choices assess new people at the known design. They do not establish performance at new distances or under different stimulus timing. The small RMSE differences are not paired significance tests, and the six overlapping training sets are not six independent experiments.

The post hoc visual criteria require the fitted 10–90% change within the observed range, two sampled positions on either side of the midpoint, a position in the 20–80% transition, and sufficient departure from a line. These criteria make the display requirement explicit. Their numerical cutoffs and the 4.5-cm scale floor have no independently validated physiological status. A bound-hitting estimate reports an assumption-sensitive approximation.

Completed follow-up checks and remaining evidence limits

The post-results remaining-analysis amendment now addresses the feasible shape checks on the 40-record cohort excluding both ambiguous recordings:

  • Log distance: 160 pipelines receive the same summaries and smoothers as lines, with physical-distance smoothing and a log-distance response. All five families use the same nested person/position target. Visible sigmoids improve on a constant by only 0.105 ms; log distance does not improve prediction. This compares response shapes and cannot reproduce P3's expectancy control.
  • Geometry sensitivity: nine fixed specifications cross three width floors with permissive/existing/strict coverage, flank sampling and curvature criteria. All are reported. Their descriptive leave-position RMSE spans 47.47–94.59 ms; the poorest permissive specification is retained, not discarded.
  • Selection stability: 30 whole-person resamples repeat all 320 visible-method selections, keeping repeated copies together in validation folds. They select 27 distinct methods; the most frequent appears three times. This diagnoses instability, not selection-adjusted confidence coverage.
  • Individual identification: the individual extension includes known-signal recovery, refit intervals and repeated split halves, now also for Condition, Phase and interaction contrasts. Midpoint-change rank reliability is approximately zero. All 72 mean-contrast and 216 main association intervals across plateau and linkage specifications include zero. See individual parameters.

The finite checks do not establish a physiological sigmoid. Calibrated method-selection-adjusted intervals, serial-dependence calibration, formal profile-likelihood regions and recovery under a wider range of generating processes remain unestablished. These are additional research designs, not hidden completed evidence. Existing predictions condition on participant-wide cleaning; prospective forecasting would need preprocessing learned without the omitted position and a deployable target. The present report makes the narrower cleaned-profile claim.

Source recording identity, timing/breathing traces, stronger full-text coverage, a functioning Consensus connector and independent dense-distance/expectancy controls remain unavailable. Further smoothing cannot resolve those limits.

Wording suitable for the paper

We treated the sigmoid as a theory-motivated descriptive candidate and assessed sensitivity to robust extraction, smoothing and shape restrictions using one common procedure across conditions. We retained the primary analysis and participant-level fit gate, disclosed all candidate pipelines, and compared relative-profile predictions in held-out participants with equally processed linear fits and a constant. This exploratory evaluation conditions on the initial cleaned profiles. The resulting constrained curves illustrate the consequences of explicit shape assumptions; their fitted midpoints and slopes do not establish identified individual or physiological PPS boundaries.
