MAIA-2 and spatial frame of reference: review and analysis rationale

Reviewed 16 October 2026, UTC. The goal is to analyse every released data domain, including interoceptive self-report and spatial-frame ratings, with auditable scoring and uncertainty. This focused narrative review supports a post hoc extension; it is not a systematic review or a preregistration.

The three closest analysis precedents

  1. Mehling et al. (2018), MAIA-2 development. The full paper describes eight dimensions, the revised 37-item instrument, internal consistency, inter-scale correlations and factor analyses in 1,090 museum visitors. Table 2 provides the final item ordering. This supports retaining the eight dimensions; it does not validate a new pooled score or factor structure in our 41 respondents.
  2. Eggart, Todd and Valdés-Stauber (2021), applied MAIA-2 psychometrics. This study in major depression reports alpha/omega, change sensitivity and clinical change thresholds. Reliability varied by subscale and occasion, including weaker Noticing and Not-Distracting at baseline. This supports reporting sample-specific reliability. The sample was hospitalized patients (110 enrolled; 87 pre/post); its clinical thresholds and change analyses do not transfer to our single MAIA record per person. Access: indexed abstract, not full methods.
  3. Hanley, Dambrun and Garland (2020), spatial frames and body boundaries. A randomized mindfulness study in 45 healthy young adults used five sessions and repeated self-report measures. Its reported changes in spatial frames motivate examining this experiential outcome here. It does not establish a breath-VR effect, a PPS boundary, or our A–H anchor direction. Its repeated-session ANOVA and mediation cannot simply be copied into this paired-condition dataset. Access: indexed abstract, not its instrument or full analysis details.

The original MAIA study (Mehling et al., 2012; indexed abstract) also describes eight constructs and a mixed-method development process. Garfinkel et al. (2015) distinguish self-reported interoceptive sensibility, objective task accuracy and metacognitive awareness in 80 people. Its indexed abstract supports keeping these constructs distinct: our MAIA scores are not heartbeat-detection accuracy, measured respiratory compliance or evidence of a physiological mechanism.

McNeish (2018), inspected at abstract level, cautions that alpha has restrictive assumptions and discusses alternatives including omega. We report raw alpha with uncertainty as an internal-consistency diagnostic, not a validity certificate or automatic cutoff. Factor-based omega also needs a defensible measurement model. We do not fit a new 37-item factor model or delete items to optimize reliability in 41 respondents. Strong latent- structure or cross-language invariance claims need separate validation. This is our modelling judgment, not a universal minimum-n rule attributed to a paper.

Scoring and parameters retained

MAIA-2 items use 0–5. Following the development paper's final map and reversal direction, compute means for Noticing (1–4), Not-Distracting (5–10), Not-Worrying (11–15), Attention Regulation (16–22), Emotional Awareness (23–27), Self-Regulation (28–31), Body Listening (32–34) and Trusting (35–37). Reverse 5–10, 11, 12 and 15 as 5 − response. The main paper was accessible; the supplementary questionnaire and official UCSF site were not. Independent recomputation matches all 328 stored subscale scores (41 × 8). This checks numerical agreement, not the language, wording or timing of the administered form.

Retain each subscale's n, missingness, mean/SD, median/IQR, range, floor/ceiling counts, alpha with a participant-bootstrap interval, and corrected item–rest correlations. Export all 37 raw response frequency distributions. Require complete constituent items for each subscale, with no imputation or fallback to stored scores. The release has one fully missing questionnaire record and no partially completed MAIA forms. Missing questionnaires do not exclude behavioural data.

For spatial frames, retain category frequencies, the 8 × 8 transition table and within-person higher/equal/lower counts. An exact sign test uses change direction without assuming equal distances between pictures. Report ties, the higher fraction among non-ties with an exact binomial interval, and paired dominance (higher − lower) / all pairs with a whole-person bootstrap interval. Show order-stratified counts; these are descriptions, not a powered test of period or carryover effects. Higher A–H codes are not labelled allocentric until the administered anchor key is verified.

An ordinal regression could extend this analysis in a larger, well-documented design. Sparse categories, 41 pairs and unverified administration/anchor details make the exact directional comparison a useful minimal analysis here. No paired normal-mean test assumes that A–H steps are equal physical or psychological units.

Associations and multiplicity

Before calculating the new associations, the contract fixed 33 comparisons: eight dimensions × four outcomes (Control spatial rating, Viscereality rating, paired rating-change direction and raw-RT SOA-slope interaction), plus rating-change direction versus the behavioural interaction. The latter contrast is (Viscereality Inhale − Exhale) − (Control Inhale − Exhale) in slopes through five participant cell means, in ms per primary SOA SD. It does not estimate a PPS boundary.

Spearman rho uses complete participant pairs and average ranks. Use 19,999 permutations under the independence null, a two-sided absolute-statistic tail and the plus-one p-value rule. All 33 p values are reported without multiple-testing correction; undefined tests remain unestimated. The plus-one rule is part of Monte Carlo permutation inference, not adjustment across tests. The project owner requested unadjusted reporting after inspecting the initial results, replacing the initially specified Holm adjustment. This amendment does not make the analyses preregistered or confirmatory. Pointwise 95% intervals use 2,000 paired-person bootstrap draws, re-ranking within each draw; degenerate draws are excluded and counted. Intervals are not simultaneous. Permutation inference assumes exchangeable people under independence and does not control demographic, order or other confounding. The behavioural contrast's measurement uncertainty is not corrected by ranking.

The installed SciPy 1.17.1 documentation was inspected locally: Spearman guidance recommends permutation inference for smaller samples; permutation documentation explains the plus-one correction and alternative two-sided conventions. Online documentation hosts were blocked. This is a local software-reference inspection, not another paper retrieval. Our absolute-tail convention is explicit; it is not assumed identical to SciPy's default doubled-smaller-tail convention.

The spatial condition comparison is a separate, single exploratory test family. The 28 inter-subscale correlations are descriptive, with no additional p-value screening. No median splits, significance-driven subscale/covariate selection, or attenuation correction using uncertain alpha estimates is performed.

Findings and interpretation

There are 41 questionnaire records; all 42 people remain in the primary behavioural cohort. All 328 MAIA scores match. Raw alpha is 0.565 for Noticing and 0.589 for Emotional Awareness; the other six range from 0.704 to 0.861. Intervals are reported and no item is discarded because of these estimates.

Spatial ratings are higher under Viscereality for 22 people, lower for 6 and tied for 13: exact two-sided p=0.00372. Among 28 non-ties, the higher fraction is 0.786 (exact 95% CI 0.590–0.917); paired dominance is 0.390 (percentile CI 0.171–0.610). Both condition medians are D, illustrating why a median alone misses paired change. Higher/equal/lower counts are 13/6/2 for Control-first and 9/7/4 for Viscereality-first; these do not rule out period/carryover effects.

All 33 associations are reported without multiple-testing correction; four have unadjusted p<.05. Among the 24 MAIA–spatial comparisons, Trusting correlates with Viscereality ratings (rho=0.322, pointwise 95% CI 0.004–0.602, p=0.04255) and with rating-change direction (rho=0.335, CI 0.025–0.606, p=0.03445). Direction means lower/same/higher, not the magnitude of change. These related outcomes are not independent replication. Self Regulation (rho=−0.357, CI −0.603 to −0.050, p=0.02310) and Body Listening (rho=−0.312, CI −0.545 to −0.018, p=0.04740) correlate with the raw-RT slope interaction. Each association uses 41 complete pairs. Every estimate, unadjusted p value and pointwise interval is exported. These post hoc associations are exploratory, with multiple comparisons and confounding limiting inference. They do not rescue missing participant PPS boundaries or establish an interoceptive mechanism; non-significance does not establish independence.

Search and evidence limits

Six peer-reviewed works were retained: one full article and five indexed abstracts. Consensus MCP and a general web-search connector were unavailable. The multi-source skill guided public PubMed discovery, refinement to applied MAIA-2 validation and original-source retrieval. Broad questionnaire/spatial searches were refined to the named primary studies. Full-text attempts for Hanley and Eggart, the MAIA supplement, and searches on ordinal/metric pitfalls and permutation p values returned challenges; they supply no claimed full-paper evidence. The evidence ledger records every URL, actual access, hashes, included claims and limitations.

This bounded search is not an exhaustive survey through 2026. The next documentary needs are the original registration, administered pictogram key and MAIA language/timing record. Computation can be reviewed independently of these unresolved construct and provenance questions.
